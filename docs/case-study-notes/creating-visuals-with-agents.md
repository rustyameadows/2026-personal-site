## What this page is

Creating Visuals with Agents is an ongoing collection of independent visual experiments Rusty has made by working conversationally with AI agents and image-generation systems.

The page is not:

- one unified project;
- a collection with one shared visual style;
- a formal art-direction methodology;
- a process manual;
- several individual case studies disguised as one page;
- a wordless image dump;
- or a text-heavy essay with a few illustrations attached.

It should contain substantial, interesting writing and at least equal visual emphasis. Each featured experiment needs enough context to preserve what makes it notable, but none needs a complete problem/process/outcome treatment.

The only required relationship among the pieces is that Rusty started them and made them by working with agents.

## Central observation supplied by Rusty

Rusty's central belief is that text and reasoning models are remarkably good at using image-generation models.

When he watches Codex or a capable GPT model use ImageGen, the agent appears to operate with intent. It can move from a rough brief to a concept document, divide the direction into perhaps twenty-four independent prompts, run them in parallel, inspect the resulting field, understand how its own instruction missed, revise the language, and run another pass. This feels more analytical and deliberate than directly sending many prompts into an image model and hoping one lands.

The same agent can continue beyond generation into local image manipulation, masking, alignment, compositing, timing, animation, and production. Rusty has access to the same underlying image models, but the reasoning agent's ability to plan and operate them changes the breadth, speed, and coherence of what he can explore.

Everything included begins with Rusty at a blank spot. He originates the experiment, supplies references or markup when useful, reacts, rejects, redirects, selects, and keeps grinding. Do not turn this into a defensive human-versus-machine authorship argument. The compelling point is the creative loop and the work that came out of it.

## Initial examples

### Orbs

Orbs began with three reference images from different artists. Rusty then spent many rounds describing what he wanted while the agent generated images, manipulated them locally, composited them, animated them, changed timing and speed, and produced a very large family of studies.

Rusty is not presenting Orbs because he considers every final result perfect or because he loves the current project presentation. It is included because it is an unusually complete example of how far a visual idea can travel when he directs an agent that can operate image generation and production tools together.

The detailed public archive already preserves the full experiment and should be linked rather than reproduced on the personal-site page:

- Public archive: `https://orb.rustymeadows.com/archive/`
- Clean repository: `/Users/rustymeadows/Dev/Orb`
- Retained record: Reference plus R00 through R12

The archive documents image generation, local transformations, geometry and registration work, compositing, palette transfer, timing, behavioral states, and hundreds of motion outputs. Adam Fuhrer's RGB sphere study is a documented reference; identify and credit the other two reference artists during the later asset/provenance round.

### Little Plains watercolors

The Little Plains color controls began as dots, moved into Xs, and then opened up when Rusty asked, “What about watercolors?” Rusty and the agent explored multiple visual families and motion directions, generated the individual marks, processed them, and turned them into a production system.

This example must preserve the fact that the work shipped. It was not only a set of attractive generated swatches. The final family contains twelve independently authored watercolor marks with sixteen baked states each—192 frames—integrated as interactive color controls on the Little Plains website. The implementation includes exact resting frames, cleaned transparency, deterministic drift corrections, loading behavior, motion behavior, reduced-motion handling, and fallbacks.

The Little Plains project case study can explain the broader website. Creating Visuals with Agents should emphasize the remarkable path from an offhand visual idea to a coherent set of generated images, animations, and real production assets.

### Little Plains editorial illustrations

Rusty is currently working with ChatGPT and Codex to make illustrations for Little Plains's article about the new website. These belong to a different visual and editorial context than Orbs or the watercolor controls. They do not need to match either one.

### Synthetic modeling agency

In an earlier experiment, Rusty generated an entire modeling agency so he could have better models available for image-making. This may become a smaller historical entry or gallery beat after the artifacts are located and reviewed.

Its potential importance is the move from generating one finished image to generating reusable visual ingredients: a cast of people that can support future images. Do not force this connection into the launch page if the work does not hold up visually.
